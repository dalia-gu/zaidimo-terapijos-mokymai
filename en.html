Why learn play therapy

If you work with children and feel that talking or familiar methods do not always help, that is understandable. Young children cannot yet tell us what is difficult for them. They show it through play.

Play therapy is a form of psychotherapy particularly well suited to children. A professionally trained play therapist uses play as the primary means of helping a child overcome emotional, behavioural and other psychological difficulties (Schaefer, 1999). Play is the child's natural language, and toys are their words (Landreth, 2002).

It is a research-based approach. Meta-analyses show high to moderate effectiveness of play therapy, and humanistic, non-directive approaches show better outcomes than other orientations (Bratton et al., 2005).

Our play therapy training programme is the only one in Lithuania. It follows international guidelines for the training of play therapists and is based on the approach of Dr Garry Landreth and other leading figures in child-centred play therapy.

Five beliefs of child-centred play therapists

Non-directive play therapy rests not on techniques, but on a way of seeing the child.

  • Language of play

    Play is the child's natural language, and toys are their words.

  • The child knows

    Children have an inner wisdom about how to move towards their own growth and healing.

  • The child leads

    The child leads the therapeutic process, and the therapist follows.

  • Acceptance

    Children are complete, resilient individuals worthy of respect, not miniature adults.

  • Relationship

    The relationship itself is the primary vehicle for change. Process matters more than outcome.

Frequently asked questions

These questions come up for almost everyone considering the programme. If yours is not here, please write to us.

info@menaspadeti.lt

Can I combine the training with work?

Sessions will take place at weekends, roughly every two months, so no working days are needed. The play therapy practice can usually be done at your own workplace.

What do I need to complete to receive the certificate?

Attend all modules and complete the practical assignments, supervised play therapy practice and the final exam.

Play therapy practice begins in the autumn, once you already have the core practical skills.

Is the programme accredited?

The programme is registered in the Lithuanian Register of Non-Formal Education Programmes and is currently being assessed for inclusion in Kursuok.lt.

Full accreditation, as in other countries, will only become possible once the relevant legal framework and a Play Therapy Association are in place in Lithuania.

Every participant in the training will be able to contribute to the recognition and accreditation of play therapy in Lithuania and abroad.

Which languages are used?

The training will be delivered in Lithuanian. When a guest lecturer from abroad teaches, the session may be in English, with interpretation provided if needed.

Because of the shortage of Lithuanian-speaking play therapy supervisors, and to keep external supervision independent of the training organisers, the only way to receive it is online with specialists from other countries.

We will help you find and choose an external supervisor from our list of recommended supervisors. The main language is English, with the option of Polish or Slovak. We will also try to arrange supervision in Russian.

You may also find a supervisor yourself, provided their qualifications meet the requirements for play therapy supervision.

Who can apply

The programme is for experienced professionals who want to work therapeutically with children and families, work with parents and use creative therapeutic methods.

We accept psychologists, social workers, doctors, psychotherapists, art and family therapists who hold a master's degree and have at least 2–3 years of experience working with children.

For the admission procedure, required documents and fees, please contact info@menaspadeti.lt.
